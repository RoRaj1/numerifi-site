Protecting Yourself & Setting Goals

Spot common scams and set a SMART financial goal. ~15 min

Insurance basics

Insurance exists because large, rare losses are hard for any one person to absorb, but predictable across a large group. A pool of people each pay a small, regular amount (a premium); the pool covers the few people who have a large loss in any given period. You're not "wasting money" on insurance you never use — you're paying to transfer the risk of a loss you couldn't otherwise cover. Health insurance, car insurance, and renters/homeowners insurance all work on this same pooling principle, just for different kinds of risk.

Common scams & identity theft

Scams succeed by creating pressure that skips past normal judgment: a fake emergency, a deadline in minutes, a threat of arrest or account closure. Identity theft is a specific version of this — someone using your personal information (Social Security number, card numbers, login credentials) to open accounts or make charges in your name. The best defense is slowing down: a legitimate organization will not pressure you to act in the next five minutes, and will not ask you to pay in gift cards.

Urgency and pressure. "Act now or your account will be closed" is designed to stop you from thinking it through.

Payment by gift card or wire transfer. No legitimate tax agency, utility, or employer asks to be paid this way.

An offer that's too good to be true. Guaranteed high returns, "double your money," or a prize you never entered for.

An unexpected request for personal information. A real bank or agency won't ask you to confirm a password or full SSN over a text or call they initiated.

If it happens to you: stop contact immediately, don't send anything further, and report it — to your bank or card issuer if money or card numbers were involved, and to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov. Acting fast limits the damage.

SMART goals & net worth

A financial goal is easier to hit when it's SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. "I want to save money" is a wish. "I want to save $500 toward a laptop by June" is a goal you can actually track and hit.

Net worth is a simple snapshot of where you stand financially: everything you own (assets: cash, savings, anything of value) minus everything you owe (liabilities: debt, loans). It's normal for net worth to be low or even negative early on, especially with student loans or a car loan. What matters isn't the number today; it's whether it's trending up over time.
